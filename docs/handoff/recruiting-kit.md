# P0-M1 Recruiting Kit — Real Cohort

> Execution aid for `docs/handoff/marketing-brief.md` P0-M1. Not a spec; delete or
> rewrite freely once the cohort is done. Terminology follows the brief: pitch, hand
> off, publish, match, explanation, invitation, mutual acceptance. Never "compatibility,"
> "score," "algorithm," or "matchmaking" — including out loud in a session.

## 0. Before anyone messages anyone

Each of the 2-4 people doing outreach fills this in first — 10 minutes, on paper or in
a shared doc. The bottleneck is naming specific specialists, not sending messages.

| Your name | 5-8 people you can personally ask | Field (must vary across the group) |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Aim for the mix the brief calls for: a deep, current, unfinished problem, not a job
title. "My friend who does photography" is not enough — you need "my friend who's been
obsessing over how shoulder-line reads in low-light portraits for three months." If you
can't name the specific current problem, ask them before you invite them, not during.

## 1. Personal-network outreach (send today, one person at a time, never a group blast)

**EN**

> Hey [name] — I'm building something at [hackathon] and thought of you specifically
> because of [their actual specific current work/problem]. It has your AI assistant
> (ChatGPT or Claude) write a short intro of you based on what you actually talk to it
> about, then matches you with one other person working on something oddly close — you
> both get an explanation of why. Takes about 15 minutes, needs you to have ChatGPT or
> Claude open. Can you do it live with me for 15 min [today/tonight], and would you be
> OK if I quote your pitch and match in our submission?

**ZH（繁體）**

> [名字]，我在做一個 hackathon 專案，第一個想到你，因為[對方目前具體在鑽研的問題]。
> 做法是請你平常在用的 AI 助理（ChatGPT 或 Claude）根據你們實際聊過的內容，寫一段介
> 紹你的文字，然後系統會配對一位在想類似問題的人，並說明為什麼配對這兩人。大概 15
> 分鐘，需要你手邊有 ChatGPT 或 Claude。[今天/今晚] 方便花 15 分鐘讓我陪你走一次嗎？
> 也想問一下，如果在提交作品時引用你的介紹和配對結果，你願意嗎？

## 2. Hackathon-community post (lower touch, broader reach, expect a lower completion rate than personal asks — use to fill gaps in field variety, not as the primary channel)

**EN**

> Looking for 15 min of your time + your ChatGPT/Claude open. We're testing whether an
> AI assistant can write your intro and find you one person worth talking to, based on
> what you actually think about — not a profile you fill in. DM me if you have a
> specific, current, deep problem in your field (any field) and want to try it live.

**ZH（繁體）**

> 想找幾位願意花 15 分鐘、手邊有開 ChatGPT 或 Claude 的人。我們在測試讓 AI 助理根據
> 你實際聊過的內容寫介紹，並配對一位可能有共同話題的人——不是填表單。如果你在某個領
> 域有具體、正在進行中的問題，想直接試試看，歡迎私訊我。

## 3. Live session — facilitator script (45 min, 6-10 people, 1 guide per 2-3 people)

Run this instead of sending a link and hoping. It fixes onboarding drop-off, collects
consent on the spot, and turns mutual acceptance from an async multi-day wait into
something that happens in the room.

1. **0-5 min — Frame it once, for the group.** What it does (own words, brief's
   positioning), why you're asking them specifically, that publishing needs their
   explicit approval and nothing goes anywhere before that. Do not describe the
   explanation step as more than the product currently does.
2. **5-10 min — Sign in.** Email OTP. Help anyone whose OTP email is slow.
3. **10-25 min — Hand off.** Each person opens their assistant, pastes the prompt,
   replies to the one exclusion question, gets JSON back. This is the step people get
   stuck on — sit with whoever is behind.
4. **25-30 min — Paste, edit, publish.** Watch them actually read what publish means
   before they tap it — that's the approval gate the brief cares about capturing
   honestly.
5. **30-35 min — Matches land.** Up to ~3 min passive wait. Everyone opens their match
   at roughly the same time.
6. **35-40 min — Read explanations aloud, send invitations.** Since everyone's still in
   the room, invitations sent now can be accepted now instead of days later.
7. **40-45 min — Capture, right here, before anyone leaves:**
   - Verbal or written **"yes, you can quote my pitch/match in the submission"** — write
     down who said yes.
   - The two one-liners from the brief: *did the pitch describe you accurately?* and
     *would you actually message the person you were shown?*

## 5. Guardrail

If a participant asks "does the AI actually explain why we're a good match, or is that
canned?" — answer honestly. The brief's contingency section says the PM hasn't
confirmed yet whether the real model-generated explanation has shipped. Don't promise
either way in front of a cohort member; if asked, say it's being finalized.
